The meaning

The name carries
ancient instruction.

Tshivenda is one of South Africa's 11 official languages, spoken primarily in Limpopo. The closest known root — Tendani — carries one of the most audacious meanings a name can hold.

Three independent South African user submissions agreed: it means "Believe in God." A fourth translates it as "Agree as a statement or instruction." Not a passive noun. A command. A calling.

A child spoke this into an adult who had spent years studying belief, behaviour, and systems — without yet fully believing in himself. The timing was not accidental.

Beyond the highlight reel

The years this story
almost didn't survive to tell.

There was a stretch of years the photos above can only hint at. A business that didn't survive a season no one could plan for. A home that disappeared. Possessions, routines, certainty — gone, faster than I could track. I spent time in hospital fighting my way back from places that don't always let you return, and time after that with nothing: no roof, no plan, no map back to who I'd been.

What carried me through wasn't a strategy. It was strangers who had no reason to help and helped anyway. An old man in a hospital ward, tied to his bed and thirsty — I gave him water because it was the only thing I could do that day. Days later, in a way I never expected and still can't fully explain, that small act came back around and helped save my own life. A man outside a shop asked where I was headed, and simply drove me there. A family took me in when I had nothing to offer but my word — and kept it anyway.

I lost relationships in that chapter that I thought were permanent. Not because anyone was the villain of the story — collapse doesn't ask permission, and it doesn't spare the people standing closest to you when it lands. Everyone involved, including me, was doing the best they could with what they had left to do it with. I'm choosing to hold that without bitterness, because bitterness is one more weight I can't afford to carry uphill.

What rebuilt me wasn't a single turning point — it was small, repeated proof that I was still capable of something. A skill learned from nothing. A debt repaid. A hand reached back to someone standing exactly where I once stood. Slowly: a roof again. A relationship with my mother and my sister, restored. Work — from people who'd known me at my lowest and chose to trust me anyway. A craft: I taught myself to build. This very page was built by the same hands that once had nothing left to build with.

I'm not finished becoming. But I am here — present, working, reachable — and there was a year where that alone would have sounded impossible. That's the real foundation under everything else on this page: not the wins, but the proof that a person can lose almost everything and still choose, one ordinary day at a time, to rebuild.

When time fractured

The Moment Everything Stopped Making Sense

There are moments in life where time does not move forward in a straight line. It folds. It compresses. It fractures identity. There was a period where survival became the only operating system — not success, not ambition. Survival.

Everything collapsed at once. Financial stability, professional identity, the relationships that had held shape for years — all of it disintegrated under pressure that had been building long before I could name it.

  • Financial stability collapsed
  • Professional identity fragmented
  • Relationships dissolved under the weight
  • Trust became difficult to maintain — both ways

And yet something unusual happened inside that collapse. I started observing patterns instead of resisting them. Not because I was strong. But because I had no energy left to fight reality. That is where the shift began.

The one structure that held

The Mother Architecture

At the lowest point of system collapse, there is usually one remaining structure that holds identity together. For me, that structure was my mother — Joey Mary. The love of my life. Not as an emotional concept — but as a real, living operational support system who chose to hold the door open when every other door had closed.

  • Shelter when there was none
  • Food when systems failed
  • A computer when I had no tools left
  • Belief when belief had disappeared

That computer became the first gateway back into structure. It was not just a device — it became the interface between collapse and reconstruction. It introduced me to systems thinking again. And eventually to AI, which became the first non-judgmental mirror I had encountered in years.

Not motivation. Repetition.

The Reconstruction Phase

Rebuilding did not happen through inspiration. It happened through repetition. Small systems. Small wins. Small re-entry points into structure:

  • Writing again · Coding again · Analysing again
  • Building again · Losing again · Adjusting again

The difference this time: I stopped attaching identity to outcome. And started attaching identity to process. That one shift changed the architecture of every decision that followed.

Not all endings were permanent

The Return of Relationships

Not all relationships ended in rupture. Some returned. Some healed. Some evolved into something stronger for having survived the break. A key reconnection happened with Stefan Van Eeden — Oom Stefan — a man who had opened the very first door in business before I had earned it. The one who chose the name "Prime Turf." The one who believed in me when the evidence said otherwise. And the one I had failed to honour properly during the collapse years.

The message I sent was simple: "I'm sorry. And thank you." The response was immediate — no resentment, no accounting of past damage, no ledger. Just forward movement. That is the nature of a real mentor. That message opened the door to everything Prime Turf became.

Real relationships do not store debt. They store truth. Stefan Van Eeden proved that.

Continuity across the chaos

The Children — The Stability Anchor

In the middle of instability, one truth remained constant: my children were still becoming who they were becoming. Regardless of my circumstances. Their development became a grounding point in chaos — not as responsibility alone, but as perspective.

They represent continuity, future orientation, and emotional grounding — identity beyond collapse. Each one reflecting a different dimension of resilience: achievement, competitiveness, creativity, independence. They are not symbols. They are lived reality.

The reframe

The Shift in Understanding

At some point, I stopped asking "Why did this happen to me?" and started asking "What does this structure reveal?" That shift changed everything.

  • Failure became feedback
  • Collapse became information
  • Relationships became systems
  • Emotion became data

Life stopped being random. It became readable. And once you can read a system — even a broken one — you can redesign it. That is the foundation of everything I build now.
